## Briefing Lab scrolls without the pauses

User report: "Briefing Lab scroll 할 때 종종 버벅이는데, 이거 좀 개선 안되나?"

- Measured first. Scrolling the feed's own markup is not the problem: a page holding thirty
  briefings, 36,909 elements and a scroller 152,078 px tall scrolls at a median frame of 13.3 ms
  with one long frame in 120. The cost is React, not paint.
- The feed rendered every briefing that was ever kept. `loadAllHistory` pages until the server stops,
  and the list rendered all of it, so one re-render of this view cost 394 ms at thirty briefings and
  857 ms at sixty, even when nothing had changed. Any re-render (a briefing finishing, guidance
  arriving, collapsing the sections, saving feedback, marking mail read) froze the window for that
  long, which is what was felt while scrolling.
- The feed now keeps the eight newest briefings in the page and brings eight more in as the reader
  reaches the end, with a button that does the same for anyone who prefers it. Grouping, which walks
  every item of every briefing, is computed once per history instead of once per render.
- The same measurement after the change: 113 ms at thirty briefings and 167 ms at sixty, and the
  cost no longer grows with how much history is kept.
- A notification always reaches its own briefing: when it points at one below the window, the window
  opens far enough to include it, so the jump still lands. What the reader has already brought in
  stays in on a re-render.

### Per-server AI notes for SSH servers

- What the user wants an AI to keep in mind on one registered server, for example the account to run
  jobs under on a shared machine. It is kept apart from the connection profile on purpose: the
  profile's version is part of every trusted-access approval, so editing a note there would revoke
  the user's approvals for that server.
- [Contracts](../../apps/desktop/src/shared/ssh-agent-notes-contracts.ts) (2,000 characters a note,
  200 servers, control characters refused),
  [store](../../apps/desktop/src/main/ssh-agent-notes-store.ts) (`ssh-agent-notes.v1.json` in the
  user data folder, notes of servers that no longer exist dropped),
  [IPC](../../apps/desktop/src/main/ssh-agent-notes-ipc.ts), and `sshAgentNotes.get`/`set` in the

### Verbatim `doi` and `url` in the Literature BibTeX export

- `serializeEntry` escaped every field, so a Springer chapter DOI left as
  `10.1007/978-3-030-58452-8\_13`. LaTeX reads these two fields verbatim (`\doi{}`, `\url{}`,
  biblatex verbatim fields), so that backslash was printed and the link broke.
  The [exporter](../../apps/desktop/src/main/literature-bibtex.ts) now writes `doi` and `url` with
  whitespace, braces and backslashes removed and nothing escaped; every other field is still escaped.
  Backslashes go too, because a kept one reads back as an escape and a trailing one swallows the
  closing brace, which made GOSU's own import refuse the whole file.
- The importer was not changed: a file from an earlier GOSU version (`\_`, `\&`, `\%`, `\#`) and a
  new one read to the same DOI and URL, and re-exporting either writes the new form.
  [Tests](../../apps/desktop/test/literature-bibtex.test.ts) 13 (+6): the export of an identifier
  holding `_`, `%`, `#` and `&`, the round trip of both forms, and that every other field keeps its
  escapes. Four of the six failed against the old exporter; the other two were confirmed against
  deliberately broken copies of the module.
- This is what the [paper-summary export](../../apps/briefing-lab/src/paper-library-bibtex.ts)
  already did, and it closes the follow-up left open in the [0.58.141 record](0.58.141.md).
- Bundle inspection of the installed app: the serializer in `app.asar` writes those two fields
  through the verbatim path.
